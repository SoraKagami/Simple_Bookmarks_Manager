- **What TreeMaps does:** it builds temporary lookup tables for the current bookmark tree, mainly `id → node`, `parentId → children`, and folder-related subsets. `Map` lookups are intended to avoid repeated recursive tree walking; JS `Map` is designed for sublinear average lookup access. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://developer.mozilla.org&sz=128)MDN Web Docs](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript/Reference/Global_Objects/Map?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- **What it should impact:** actions that repeatedly ask “find this bookmark/folder,” “get this folder’s children,” or “which folders are visible?” Examples: Library rendering, Library search, expand/collapse-all, keyboard navigation, and drag auto-expand.
- **Why “on” can feel slower:** with TreeMaps on, SBM does an upfront indexing pass, then rendering can begin more predictably. That can feel like steadier/progressive work rather than a single delayed refresh. The user-visible perception may be “slower” even if total time is similar or slightly better.
- **Why “off” can feel snappier:** without maps, SBM skips the upfront indexing allocation. For small/medium trees or simple actions, direct traversal may be cheaper, so the visible render starts after a short pause and then completes quickly.
- **Your observation matches expectations:** TreeMaps is most likely to help on large bookmark trees and repeated lookup-heavy operations. It may not help, or may feel worse, when the tree is modest, browser JS engine caches are warm, or the bottleneck is DOM rendering/layout rather than tree lookup. Browsers only have about 16.7 ms per frame at 60 FPS for script/style/layout/paint, so small differences in when work happens can change perceived responsiveness.
